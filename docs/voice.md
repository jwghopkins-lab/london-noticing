# Voice

**This file is yours. Overwrite anything in it.**

`docs/house-style.md` is a list of rules with a checker behind it. It can tell
you a sentence is 26 words or that you said the same thing twice. It cannot tell
you whether a line sounds like you.

So this file is for the part a checker cannot hold: what the walks should sound
like. It is seeded below from corrections you have actually made, so you are
editing a draft rather than facing a blank page. Everything here is a guess at
what you meant until you say otherwise.

## The most useful thing you can put here

Pairs. Something I wrote, and the same thing in your words. Three or four good
pairs are worth more than a page of adjectives, because they show the move
rather than describing it.

```
Mine:   That is the third living this hill has made.
Yours:  The town has made its living three ways.
```

Second most useful: a short list of words and constructions you never want to
see, and a short list you like. Those can go straight into the checker.

## Seeded from your corrections

Each of these came from something you cut or reworded. Correct them freely — I
have guessed at the principle behind each one and I will have got some wrong.

### Cut the summing-up
You removed "That is the last thing worth taking away from this walk", "The
people who did it decided that a careful copy of the truth was better than an
honest hole in the ground", and the sign-off that restated the distance and the
stop count. **Principle guessed: say the thing once and stop. Never tell the
reader what they were supposed to get out of it.**

### Nothing condescending about a place or the people in it
A city was not "flat, wet and made of clay"; it had no building stone. A port
did not "charge everybody"; it took a cut of what passed. Men in a treadwheel
were not "like hamsters". **Principle guessed: the joke must not be at the
expense of the people who lived there.**

### No riddle furniture
"Somewhere in here is a painting. Go and find it" is a puzzle. "There is a
painting of that story on the wall" is a guide. **Principle guessed: this is
somebody showing you a thing, not withholding one.**

### Shorter, and the clues too
"Make the language as plain English and non claudey and succinct as possible,
even the clues." **Principle guessed: a clue does not need a flourish, it needs
to be answerable.**

### Say it once per card
The first Castres clue said "This street is named after a courtroom" in the look
and again in the question. **Principle guessed: the walker reads the card
straight through, so write it straight through.**

### Two sentences turned against each other
This shipped at Talbot Yard and you rewrote it:

```
Mine:   The Tabard stood here. It was one of a long row along this street,
        because everybody heading south had to start from Southwark.
        The yards are still called after the inns. The inns themselves have gone.
Yours:  The Tabard inn used to be one of many pubs on this road, they are gone,
        but the yards still have the names that the pubs used to.
```

Three separate things went wrong and they are worth naming apart.

**The reversal.** "The yards are still called after the inns. The inns
themselves have gone." Same noun, then the turn, then the beat. Both sentences
are true and the pair is a figure of speech. Yours puts it in one sentence and
loses nothing. **Principle guessed: if two sentences are arranged against each
other, say it in one.** Now checked.

**The word doing nothing.** "themselves". Read the sentence without it. Now
checked, along with itself, himself and herself.

**The reason nobody asked for.** "because everybody heading south had to start
from Southwark" arrives before the reader has wondered why. Yours drops it
entirely. **Principle guessed: give the fact. A reason only earns its place
when the fact is surprising without it.**

### No walking time on a short leg
"About 23 metres, a minute or two" is nonsense; it is twenty paces.

### Round the distances
"About 296 metres" is weirdly exact.
